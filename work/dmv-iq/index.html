DMV IQ

Fifty-one driving tests, and no two of them are quite the same.

DMV IQ is built state by state, so you practice the rules of the place where you will actually take the test.

There is no national driving test. Every state, and the District of Columbia, writes its own rulebook, sets its own passing score and asks its own questions. The pattern most prep tools follow is to ignore that, hand everyone the same generic quiz, and hope the overlap is big enough.

We started from the opposite end. DMV IQ holds more than 7,500 practice questions across all fifty states and DC, and the details that differ from one state to the next are checked against each state's own material.

A permit test should be about the rules of your state, not anyone else's.

Signs first, then the road

Road signs are where many people lose easy points, so the app trains them visually: shapes, colors and meanings that stick through repetition rather than memorization. When the written test is behind you, step-by-step road test guides cover the part of the process that comes next.

Studying where it counts

Not all study time is equal. DMV IQ shows how ready you are as a simple percentage, then points you at the questions you are most likely to miss, so the hours you have go where they matter. Personalized study puts your weak spots first.

Who it is for

DMV IQ is for first-time drivers and teenagers working toward a learner's permit, new arrivals to the United States who need a state licence, adults returning to the road, and professionals preparing for a commercial driver's licence.

What is inside

Three kinds of practice for all fifty states and the District of Columbia: car, motorcycle and CDL. In total there are more than 7,500 questions, built from 51 official state handbooks. The car permit test alone has more than 3,000 questions across 13 categories, and the motorcycle test has more than 1,600 across seven. States set their own rules for the written test, and most use between 20 and 50 questions with a passing score between 70 and 80 percent, so each state has its own set.

Alongside the practice sits a library of guides: road signs, what to bring to the DMV, how many times you can retake the test, and state-by-state comparisons such as the cost of becoming a driver and the share of uninsured drivers.
