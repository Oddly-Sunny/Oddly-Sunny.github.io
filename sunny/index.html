New Jersey car test, after a missed practice question about passing an ice cream truck

I picked A. Why is that wrong?

Option A is wrong because it skips the stop. New Jersey requires you to stop first, then drive past the truck at no more than 15 mph. Slowing to 25 mph and passing with your horn does not meet the rule.

The New Jersey Driver Manual says that when an ice cream truck is flashing red lights and posting a stop signal arm, you must "Stop, and then drive past the truck at a speed of no more than 15 mph" (New Jersey Driver Manual, p. 80). It also says to yield to anyone crossing the road to or from the truck and to watch out for children.

Remember this: stop first, then 15 mph or less.

New Jersey Driver Manual, p. 80

Aviator IQPreview

Private pilot, answered from the Federal Aviation Regulations

How much fuel do I need for a VFR flight at night in an airplane?

For a VFR flight in an airplane at night, you must have enough fuel to fly to your first point of intended landing and then fly for at least 45 minutes at normal cruising speed. Wind and forecast weather count toward that fuel. (14 CFR § 91.151)

For comparison, the daytime requirement is at least 30 minutes after reaching the first landing point. Rotorcraft have a different rule, 20 minutes. (14 CFR § 91.151)

14 CFR § 91.151
Airwaves IQPreview

Amateur radio, answered from the FCC rules (47 CFR Part 97)

How often do I have to give my call sign?

You must transmit your assigned call sign at the end of each communication, and at least every 10 minutes during a communication (47 CFR § 97.119). The call sign must be sent in English if you use voice (phone), and you may use a phonetic alphabet to help others copy it.

47 CFR § 97.119
Mariner IQPreview

Coast Guard exam, answered from the Inland Navigation Rules

What lights does a power-driven vessel underway show at night?

A power-driven vessel underway shows these lights at night (33 CFR § 83.23, Rule 23):

  • A masthead light forward.
  • A second masthead light, abaft of and higher than the forward one. A vessel under 50 meters in length does not have to show this light, but may.
  • Sidelights.
  • A sternlight.

A power-driven vessel under 12 meters in length may show an all-round white light and sidelights instead of the lights above.

How it works

Sunny is our own setup, designed for test prep and education from the ground up. It does not answer from general knowledge. It answers from the sources each exam is built on.

Built on the official source

Each brand connects Sunny to the official material behind its exam: state driver handbooks, FAA and FCC regulations, Coast Guard navigation rules. Sunny searches that text before it answers.

Shows where it came from

Answers name the page or rule they come from. In DMV IQ, tapping a citation opens the state handbook at that page.

Checked before you see it

Every number in an answer has to appear in the source Sunny looked up. An answer that fails the check is repaired before it reaches the learner.

Knows the learner's test

Sunny works from the learner's own setup and progress: their state or certificate, test date and the questions they keep missing. Scores and readiness come from our scoring, never from a guess.

One engine, many exams

The core is built once and knows nothing about any single exam. Each brand plugs in its sources, exam rules and question bank, so a new exam gets the same coach without starting over.

Web, iOS and Android, in three languages

The same coach runs on the website and in the apps, and answers in English, Spanish or Chinese, keeping every number, limit and fee exactly as the source states it.

Responsible by design

  • Always presented as AI, with a reminder that the official source has the final word.
  • When the source does not cover something, Sunny says so instead of filling the gap.
  • When a question's explanation and the source disagree, Sunny goes with the source and says so.
  • Every answer can be rated or reported. Our team reviews feedback by anonymous ID, never by name or email.
  • Safety comes first. A learner who sounds in distress is pointed to real help, and Sunny never helps with anything unsafe or with cheating on a test.
